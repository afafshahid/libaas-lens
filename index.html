Perception Science

The 11.62% Ethnic vs. Western Disparity

Why deep convolutional networks perceive Western apparel with higher certainty than traditional South Asian clothing.

Western Rigidity

Geometric Invariance in Western Wear

Western garments feature fixed structural geometries: Jeans - Trousers achieved a perfect 100% Precision and 100% Recall, and Dress achieved 100% Recall. Rigid bifurcated leg seams, waistband rivets, belt loops, and heavy denim twill textures remain invariant regardless of fold or pose. Standardized silhouettes allow CNN kernels to detect Western staples with 92.82% accuracy.

Ethnic Plasticity

Fluid Drape Plasticity in Ethnic Garments

South Asian attire is defined by unstitched fabric draping and folding plasticity. An unstitched 6-yard Saree wrapped around the waist and pleated across the shoulder exhibits radical geometric variability across camera angles. In our test set, Saree samples were occasionally confused with Lehenga due to identical pleated bottom falls and heavy border zari embroidery.

Mandarin Collar Overlap

Kurta vs. Sherwani & Waistcoat Confusions

Kurtas are occasionally confused with Sherwani and Waistcoat. All three garments share identical upright mandarin band collars (*bain gala*) and structured button plackets. When downsampled to $224 \times 224$ pixels, fine fabric weight cues (light summer lawn vs heavy wedding brocade) blur, shifting model attention to the dominant collar features.

Failure Mode Resolution

Resolving the Open-Set Lehenga Bias

In 10-class models lacking a general suit category, any Pakistani female stitched dress or 3-pc lawn suit was forced into Lehenga. By curating authentic Pakistani designer imagery (Khaadi, J., Sapphire, Gul Ahmed, Alkaram) into a balanced Shalwar Kameez class (covering both male kurta pajama sets and female 2-pc/3-pc suits), Libaas Lens eliminated this systematic bias with 99%+ empirical test confidence.
